Instagram. Nothing here is a mockup: the product shots are screenshots of the

pricing engines for the demo property at 1425 Maple Ave.

**Audience for all of it:** fence contractors and fence-company owners in the
US — small crews doing residential work, 1–15 employees. They are not
shopping for software; they are tired of driving out to measure.

## 3. How to run it

**Structure.** One campaign, objective *Leads* or *Traffic* to
`fencescan.com`. Three ad sets so you can read the results:

1. **Cold — interest:** fence contractors, contractors, home improvement,
   construction-business interests; 25–60; US; Advantage+ placements on.
2. **Cold — broad:** no interests, US, 28–60. Meta's targeting usually beats
   hand-picked interests on a niche this small — give it the whole set of
   creative and let it choose.
3. **Retargeting:** site visitors 30 days + video viewers 50%+, excluding
   anyone who signed up.

**Which creative goes where.** Start cold audiences on 10, 01 and 02 (the
pain-first hooks). Put 03, 04, 07 and 09 in retargeting, where "how good is
it" beats "does this exist". 05, 06 and 08 are the ones that convert an owner
who already believes the measuring works — retargeting and warm.

**Budget.** $20–40/day per ad set for the first week. Do not judge a creative
on fewer than ~1,000 impressions or ~3 days.

**What to watch.** Hook rate (3-second views ÷ impressions) tells you if the
first two seconds work; cost per landing-page view tells you if the offer
works. If hook rate is under ~20%, swap the opener, not the whole ad.

**Compliance.** Every claim in this set is one the product already makes on
its own landing page, and the prices shown are engine output for a sample
0.38-acre lot — not a quote. Keep the "free scan" language accurate to
whatever the free plan actually includes when you launch.

### How it's built

- `src/plates/` — screenshots of the **real running app**, captured by
  `capture-plates.mjs`, which drives the landing page's five-act walkthrough
  (itself wired to the live takeoff/pricing engines) and shoots each act.
  Re-run it after a UI change and every ad picks up the new look.
- `src/kit.css` — the ad design system. Colors are lifted from
  `tailwind.config.ts` so the ads and the product read as one brand.
- `src/kit.js` — the timeline. Every ad is a pure function of time: the
  renderer calls `window.seek(ms)` per frame, so renders are deterministic and
  any frame can be pulled as a still.
- `src/v01…v10.html` — one file per video. `src/statics.html` — all ten images.

**What is a screenshot and what is a rebuild.** The takeoff, 3D and proposal
screens (videos 01–10) are screenshots of the running app. The calendar and
crew portal (05, 06, image 06) and the estimator canvas (11, 12) are rebuilt
inside the ads, because `/dashboard/calendar` and `/estimate` both sit behind
auth and `/estimate` additionally needs a live Google Maps scan — neither can
be screenshotted headlessly. Those rebuilds are not invented: the tool
labels, help text, status pills, hex colors and the grade readout are lifted
verbatim from `fence-canvas.tsx`, `calendar-board.tsx` and
`fence-estimator.tsx`, and two pieces are generated by the product's own
code — the aerial is the app's `<DemoAerial>` rendered standalone, and the
contour lines come out of `lib/fence/contours.ts` itself.

### The slope claims — what was checked, and where the line is

Video 12 is the riskiest ad in the set, because it answers "I still have to
drive out and check the grade." Every claim in it was traced to the code and
the figures re-run through the engine. What it says, and why that's the
ceiling:

**Safe to say (all verified):**
- Elevation is read at **every post position** along the drawn run — 8 ft
  apart on cedar — plus a 216-point lattice across the lot for the contour
  lines (Google Elevation API, server-side).
- The grade readout on screen is real, and the ad quotes its exact wording.
- Steps are counted (`ceil(rise / 1 ft)` past the racking limit), and the
  screen tells you the post length they need — 10′ there, 8′ elsewhere.
- Terrain moves **flat → sloped → steep on its own** and multiplies the labor
  line (1.0 / 1.18 / 1.4).
- The takeoff gains a real row: "Extended posts for stepped sections (slope)".
- The 224 LF example is engine output: $13,309 called flat vs $15,005
  measured — **$1,696**, at 10.7% avg / 19% max grade, 24 steps, 87 → 112
  crew hours.

**Do NOT say — these are false or overstated:**
- ❌ "You never need a site visit." The app itself prints *verify on site
  before digging*, and the ad repeats that line deliberately.
- ❌ "It measures your slope to the inch." It's Google's terrain model, not a
  survey. It catches macro grade; a garden wall or a swale is below its
  resolution.
- ❌ "It detects retaining walls." A sheer drop only raises a *suggestion*
  button; wall pricing is opt-in and never automatic.
- ❌ "It prices the longer posts." It counts extra step posts and charges a
  flat per-step allowance; the post *length* is a screen readout only.
- ❌ "It knows the digging." Rock and soil are exactly what elevation data
  cannot see, and the auto-classifier never picks "rocky".
- ❌ "Works automatically on every property." It needs the Elevation API
  enabled on the customer's own Google Maps key, and a lot flatter than 1 ft
  of span shows no topo at all.

Video 11 has one non-obvious constraint baked in: the drawing is **click to
place posts, not drag**, the green LF badge **does not tick up during the
draw** (it jumps when the run is finished), and only the segment being pulled
carries live footage. The ad animates all three correctly — videos 13 and 21
inherit the same choreography; if you re-cut any of them, keep it.
